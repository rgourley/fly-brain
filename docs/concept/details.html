How the fly works, in detail

What happens each session

  1. Up to eight symbols go on the table: everything the fly already owns, plus new ones drawn at random.
  2. Each symbol's six readings become a smell across 51 channels.
  3. The smell circuit, 7,443 neurons, runs on each smell for 50 milliseconds.
  4. The cells that fire in the learning centre (Kenyon cells) set the verdict: how strongly they connect to the brain's approach outputs, minus how strongly they connect to its avoid outputs.
  5. The fly buys the highest verdict it does not already own, sized by that verdict.
  6. It re-smells what it owns and sells anything it has liked less than at purchase two sessions in a row.
  7. When a trade closes, reward or punishment reaches the cells that picked it. Only those connections change.

Born preferences

These are the untrained fly's verdicts on eight setups. The gap between top and bottom is 21 times the run-to-run noise, so the ranking is real. It likes breakouts and dislikes quiet drifts.

How far a lesson travels

We rewarded one setup eight times, then measured how much of that reward showed up in other setups. The more a setup differed, the less it received. The opposite setup got 4%.

How good is it

Scored on 200 test setups it had never seen. 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect.

The fly's real wiring does no better than a random network of the same size. Both do slightly worse than feeding the six readings straight into a regression. Passing the numbers through 7,443 neurons loses a little information.

What the fly does have is a real mechanism. It learns at the same connections a living fly uses, by the same rule, and nothing in it was tuned on market results.

We expect it to lose. It is here as a baseline that every other agent should beat.
