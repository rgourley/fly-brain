How it works, every number, and what we chose

What happens each session

Six stocks go on the table. Each one's readings (RSI, position in the Bollinger band, distance from the 50-day average, volume against average, five-day return, RSI trend) become a smell across 51 receptor channels. The fly's olfactory circuit, 7,443 neurons of the 164,587 in the MaleCNS connectome, runs on each smell for 50 ms. Whichever Kenyon cells fire decide the verdict: their wiring onto the reward-side output neurons minus their wiring onto the punishment side. The best smell it does not already hold gets bought, sized by how convinced it is. Holdings are re-smelled every session, and one it has liked less than at purchase for two sessions running is sold. When a trade closes, dopamine reaches exactly the cells that were firing when it chose, and only those synapses move.

Born preferences

Before it traded anything, this fly already had a taste. These are its verdicts on eight setups with nothing learned, and the spread is 21 times the run-to-run noise. It was born a momentum trader.

How far a lesson travels

Reward one setup eight times and measure what nearby setups received. It falls off with similarity, the way a real fly's does. The opposite setup gets 4%.

The honest part

Held-out AUC on 200 labelled setups. Measured, not hoped.

The connectome adds nothing over a random expansion of the same shape, and the expansion loses a little of what was in the input. A table of six numbers predicts better than 164,587 neurons.

What the fly has that a spreadsheet does not is a mechanism that is real. It learns at the synapse flies actually use, with a rule flies actually use, and nothing anywhere is fitted to market outcomes.

Expect it to lose. The coin flip and the spreadsheet trade the same names on the same days, and their results sit next to its own.
